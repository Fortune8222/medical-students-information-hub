Know E. coli. Stop the Spread.

Most E. coli bacteria are harmless, but some strains cause serious illness. A few simple habits can keep you, your family and your community safe.

Read the facts

E. coli in three questions

Escherichia coli lives naturally in the intestines of people and animals. Trouble starts when a harmful strain gets into our food, water or hands.

🔬

What is it?

A rod-shaped, Gram-negative bacterium. Many strains are harmless gut residents, while others produce toxins that damage the intestines.

💧

How does it spread?

Through contaminated food and water, undercooked meat, raw milk, unwashed produce, and unwashed hands (the faecal-oral route).

🧴

How do we stop it?

Wash hands with soap, cook food thoroughly, drink safe water and keep raw and cooked foods apart.

When to get help: bloody diarrhoea, diarrhoea lasting more than three days, high fever, or signs of dehydration (very little urine, dry mouth, dizziness) need medical attention. Children and older adults are at higher risk.
